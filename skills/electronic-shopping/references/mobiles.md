# Mobiles

## Budget ranges (₹)
Under 10,000 · 10,000–15,000 · 15,000–20,000 · 20,000–30,000 · 30,000–50,000 · Above 50,000

## Main use (ask one, allow a second)
| Use | What matters most | What matters less |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Camera** (photos, reels, family functions) | Main sensor size and OIS, consistent colours, low light, video stabilisation, front camera for video calls | Megapixel count on its own, macro and depth cameras (usually filler) |
| **Gaming** (BGMI, Free Fire, COD) | Chipset performance, sustained performance without throttling, cooling, 120 Hz display, touch response | Camera |
| **Battery** (long days, travel, field work) | Battery mAh and real screen-on time in reviews, efficient chipset, charging speed | Peak performance |
| **For parents / elders** | Simple interface or easy mode, big clear display, loud speaker, good call quality, trusted service nearby, long software updates, no bloatware or ads | Gaming, high refresh rate |
| **Students / all-rounder** | Balance: decent chipset, battery, camera, updates | — |
| **Business / calls / WhatsApp** | Call quality, battery, dual SIM, storage | Gaming |

## Must-asks
- Who is it for (self, parent, student)?
- iPhone or Android preference? (An iPhone is only worth suggesting from about ₹45,000+ and when the buyer
  wants it; otherwise Android.)
- Minimum storage: suggest 128 GB at least; 256 GB for heavy photo and video users.

## Specs to compare (from the official India page)
Chipset · RAM / storage variant (and the exact variant on each store) · display (type, size, refresh rate,
brightness) · main camera sensor and OIS · battery mAh and charger in the box (yes/no) · charging watts ·
**Android version and the promised years of OS and security updates** · 5G bands · IP rating · weight.

## Default if the buyer doesn't know
Use "students / all-rounder", 8 GB RAM / 128 GB, and say so.

## Traps
- **Ads and bloatware** in the system UI of some budget phones; check reviews and mention it.
- **Updates**: a phone with 2 years of updates vs 4–6 years is a real difference for someone who keeps a
  phone 4 years. Use the brand's official update policy page.
- **Same name, different phone**: the Indian variant can have a different chipset from the global one.
  Use the India page.
- **5G in villages**: check the phone supports the bands the buyer's operator uses (Jio and Airtel use
  n78; Jio also uses n28, which reaches further). Say "check coverage with your operator".
- **"No charger in the box"**: add the charger cost to the price.
- **Offline-only models**: some brands sell certain models mainly through shops; check local chains.
